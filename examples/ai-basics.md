# A Friendly Intro to AI Tools

*The Massive AI Manual — companion deck*

---

## What is AI, really?

AI tools are **helpers**, not magic.

Think of them like a very well-read friend who is happy to
rephrase anything you don't understand - never condescending.

- Large language models (LLMs) predict text
- They are great at drafts, summaries, and explanations
- They can be confidently wrong, so **verify anything that matters**

## Which tool for which job?

| You want to... | Try | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Write or rephrase | A chat assistant | Plain-English drafts on request |
| Search with sources | An AI-assisted search | Answers with citations |
| Automate chores | Agents / MCP | Tools that actually *do* things |

*Rule of thumb: chat when thinking, agents when acting.*

## Asking better questions

1. **Say who it's for** - "explain this to a nurse, not a developer"
2. **Say what shape you need** - a list, a table, three steps
3. **Say what to avoid** - jargon, sales talk, abbreviations

> Weak: *tell me about blood pressure*
> Strong: *explain blood pressure basics in 5 steps for a patient handout, plain English, no jargon*

## Staying safe and private

- Never paste patient details into a public AI tool
- Check your workplace policy before any tool touches work data
- Prefer tools you can run **locally** for sensitive material
- Log out and clean your chat history now and then

<!-- note: For healthcare workers: repeat that this deck is education only, never medical advice. -->

## Keeping your setup local

Running small models on your own laptop is realistic in 2026 and
keeps data in your own hands.

- Tools like open assistant projects run offline
- Small local models are fine for drafting and note-cleaning
- Big tasks can still go to online tools - choice is power
